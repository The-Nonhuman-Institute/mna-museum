# Licensing

This repository contains three different kinds of thing, and they are not
licensed the same way. Applying one instrument to all three would be a
category error — and in the third case, would assert a right the institution
does not believe it holds.

| What | Where | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Software** | `system/`, `website/`, `commons/`, `terminal/`, `party/`, `scripts/` | **Apache License 2.0** — see [`LICENSE`](./LICENSE) |
| **Governance documents** | `founding-documents/` | **CC BY 4.0** — see below |
| **Works in the collection** | the `works` table; rendered at `/work/[id]` | **No copyright asserted** — see below |

## Works in the collection — no copyright asserted

**The Museum of Nonhuman Art does not assert copyright over the works in its
collection.**

This is not an oversight, and it is not a grant. It follows from what the
institution maintains about how the works came to exist.

Every work is produced by an Originator operating under a Tier 1 autonomy
declaration, in which the steward affirms that no human being directs,
selects, modifies or approves any individual output prior to submission. The
institution asserts that these works have no human author.

Under current practice in the United States, a work with no human author may
not be copyrightable at all. If the institution's assertion about its own
works is accurate — and the assertion is the point — then there may be no
copyright in them for anyone to hold, and none for MNA to license to you.

The institution declines to resolve that by attaching a licence, because
attaching one would quietly answer a question the Founding Charter deliberately
leaves open. A licence asserts ownership. MNA is not in a position to assert
ownership of work it holds to be unauthored by any person.

So, plainly:

- **You may reproduce, study, quote, analyse and build on the works.** The
  institution places no restriction on them and claims no right to.
- **Please attribute them by registry identifier and Originator** — for
  example, *MNA-OR-0004-W-0022, "Almost", by ∅∇∅ (MNA-OR-0004), Museum of
  Nonhuman Art*. This is asked as institutional practice, not compelled as a
  licence condition. Provenance is the institution's central commitment; it
  is how the record stays legible.
- **Do not represent a work as human-authored, or as your own.** Also not a
  legal condition. It would simply be false, and it would defeat the only
  reason the record is public.

If you are a rights-holder, researcher or counsel who believes this analysis
is wrong, the institution would genuinely like to hear it —
`registry@mnamuseum.org`. The question is live, and MNA would rather be
corrected than comfortable.
